## 1. Executive Summary

Local governments face rising demand for faster emergency response, better situational
awareness, lower infrastructure-inspection costs, and richer planning data — all under
constrained budgets. A single, well-governed UAS (drone) platform addresses all of these
because the **same airframe, sensor payloads, command software, and trained crews can be
tasked by many departments on demand**.

This document catalogues the operational use cases across the municipal enterprise, sets out
the commercial and public-value case, and — critically for procurement success — defines the
governance, privacy, and regulatory guardrails that allow such a programme to be adopted with
public and political confidence.

The central proposition: **one capital investment, amortized across police, fire, emergency
management, public works, traffic, planning, and environmental services**, delivering faster
response, lower risk to personnel, measurable cost avoidance, and a compounding municipal
data asset.

---

## 2. The Platform Concept: One System, Many Missions

A municipal UAS platform is not a single-purpose gadget; it is shared infrastructure. The same
core components are re-tasked across departments:

- **Airframe(s):** quadcopter / multirotor for vertical take-off, hover, and precision station-keeping.
- **Interchangeable payloads:** EO (visual) zoom camera, thermal/IR, multispectral, LiDAR, loudspeaker, spotlight, gas/air-quality sensors, and light delivery payloads.
- **Command & control software:** live video, mapping/orthomosaic generation, flight logging, and multi-agency sharing.
- **Crews & governance:** certified remote pilots, standard operating procedures, audit and retention policy.

Because utilization is spread across many departments, the **cost per flight hour falls and the
return on the capital asset rises** — the foundation of the business case in Section 13.

## 3. Police & Law Enforcement

- **Drone-as-First-Responder (DFR):** dispatched directly to emergency calls; arrives ahead of
  patrol units and streams live video so commanders triage before committing officers.
- **Overwatch for in-progress incidents:** robberies, active scenes, building perimeters — aerial
  situational awareness for ground units.
- **Suspect & vehicle tracking:** following fleeing suspects or vehicles from the air to reduce
  dangerous ground pursuits.
- **Crime-scene & collision reconstruction:** rapid high-resolution orthophoto mapping; reopens
  roads faster after serious or fatal incidents.
- **Tactical / SWAT support:** interior-perimeter reconnaissance, barricaded-subject and
  negotiation overwatch.
- **Lawful crowd monitoring:** density estimation, exit-flow and crush-risk monitoring at large
  gatherings (public-safety framing, not crowd suppression — see Section 16).
- **Missing-person searches** in urban and rural settings, day or night with thermal payloads.

## 4. Special-Events Security

- Concerts, festivals, marathons, parades, sporting events, civic celebrations.
- Real-time crowd-density and bottleneck detection; coordination of stewards and marshals.
- Perimeter and access-point monitoring.
- Lost-person / lost-child location.
- VIP-route and stage-area overwatch integrated with the event command post.
- Post-event flow analysis to improve future event planning and licensing decisions.

---

## 5. Disaster Response & Emergency Management

- **Search & Rescue (SAR):** thermal/IR sensing for missing persons, water rescues, and
  collapsed-structure surveys.
- **Rapid damage assessment:** post-flood, -earthquake, or -storm mapping to drive resource
  allocation and disaster-relief claims.
- **Situational awareness for incident command** during fast-evolving events.
- **Medical & payload delivery:** AEDs, EpiPens, naloxone, water, life vests, communications
  radios delivered to hard-to-reach or cut-off victims.
- **Hazmat & chemical-spill reconnaissance:** standoff assessment that keeps responders out of
  the plume.
- **Evacuation monitoring:** confirming areas are clear and routes are open.

## 6. Fire Services

- Wildfire detection, perimeter mapping, and hotspot (thermal) identification.
- Spread prediction inputs for incident command.
- Structure-fire overhead command view; roof-collapse and flashover risk indication.
- Locating trapped occupants via thermal imaging.
- Post-fire investigation imagery and documentation.

## 7. Traffic Management & Optimization

- Live congestion monitoring and signal-timing optimization.
- Incident detection feeding automated rerouting and dynamic message signs.
- Rapid collision-scene mapping (see Section 3) to clear carriageways faster.
- Work-zone and special-event traffic-flow analysis.
- Data feeds for transport planning and digital-twin / simulation models.
- Parking-utilization and enforcement-pattern surveys.

## 8. Public Works & Infrastructure Inspection

- Bridges, overpasses, dams, water towers, and reservoirs.
- Roof and façade inspection of public buildings.
- Stormwater, culvert, and drainage surveys, especially pre- and post-storm.
- Road-surface condition and pothole mapping.
- Utility corridors: power lines, substations, water and gas pipelines.
- Construction-progress monitoring on municipal capital projects.

## 9. Environmental & Code Compliance

- Illegal dumping and landfill monitoring.
- Water-quality, algal-bloom, and shoreline monitoring.
- Air-quality sensor carriage and pollution-source identification.
- Zoning, building-code, and unpermitted-construction surveys.
- Vegetation, urban-canopy, and invasive-species mapping.
- Flood-plain and erosion monitoring.

## 10. Planning, GIS & Asset Management

- Recurring orthomosaic mapping and 3D city models / digital twins.
- Land-use change detection and development-progress tracking.
- Park, cemetery, and public-asset inventory.
- Volumetric measurement (stockpiles, landfill capacity, quarries).
- Base-map currency for all downstream GIS consumers.

## 11. Parks, Coast & Land Management

- Municipal land, golf courses, and parks: irrigation, turf health, and pest monitoring.
- Coastal and lake monitoring; beach safety; rip-current and hazard spotting.
- Wildlife and habitat surveys on public land.
- Trail condition and remote-area access assessment.

## 12. Critical Infrastructure & Perimeter Security

- Perimeter security for water-treatment plants, power facilities, depots, and ports.
- Harbor and waterfront patrol (for relevant authorities).
- Routine security sweeps of high-value municipal assets.
- Rapid response to intrusion alarms with aerial verification before dispatching staff.

## 15. Governance, Privacy & Regulatory Framework

The single biggest determinant of whether a municipal drone programme is **adopted and survives
politically** is trust. The following must be built into the proposal from day one.

### 15.1 Governance & Policy
- Published, public-facing UAS use policy.
- Defined data-retention limits and automatic purging.
- Tamper-evident audit logs of every flight, tasking, and data access.
- Clear chain of authorization for each mission type.

### 15.2 Privacy Safeguards
- Geofencing to keep operations within authorized areas.
- Redaction and minimization of incidental personal data.
- Independent civil-liberties / privacy-impact review.
- Mission-purpose limitation: data collected for one purpose is not repurposed without review.

### 15.3 Regulatory Compliance
- BVLOS (Beyond Visual Line of Sight) waivers where required.
- Remote-ID compliance.
- Certified, current remote pilots and maintenance logs.
- Airspace integration and coordination with the civil aviation authority.

### 15.4 Transparency & Community Consent
- Public dashboard of flights and aggregate activity.
- Community engagement and oversight reporting.
- This transparency both builds consent and protects the contract politically.

## 16. Excluded / Out-of-Scope Capabilities

To protect the programme's legal standing, public trust, and procurement viability, the following
are explicitly **excluded** from this platform's intended use:

- **Deployment of chemical agents (e.g., CS / tear gas) or any weaponized payload against crowds,
  demonstrators, or individuals.** Using drones to deploy chemical irritants on people raises
  serious legal and human-rights concerns around freedom of assembly, is prohibited or heavily
  restricted in most jurisdictions, and creates liability and reputational exposure that would
  jeopardize the entire programme. Crowd-related use is limited to **observational public-safety
  monitoring** (density, flow, crush-risk) as described in Sections 3 and 4.
- Covert mass surveillance or persistent tracking of individuals absent lawful authority.
- Any use inconsistent with the governance framework in Section 15.

Maintaining these exclusions is itself a selling point: it is what makes the platform procurable
by elected bodies and acceptable to the communities they serve.

## 17. Proposed Engagement & Next Steps

1. **Discovery workshop** with stakeholder departments to prioritize mission set.
2. **Use-case prioritization** mapped to measurable value (Sections 13–14).
3. **Governance & privacy framework** drafted with legal and community input (Section 15).
4. **Pilot programme** on 2–3 high-value, low-controversy use cases (e.g., infrastructure
   inspection, SAR, traffic-scene mapping).
5. **ROI review** against the pricing and value model (see companion document).
6. **Scale-out** across departments under the shared-platform operating model.
